#### 3B — The step that actually found it: WHERE, not HOW MANY

Three ablations had narrowed and none had attributed. Printing the failing bytes' coordinates took **one run**:

```
row 109 col 35  got $FB want $00
row 109 col 36  got $F0 want $FB      got[c] == want[c+1]
row 110 col 34  got $02 want $00      on every affected row
```

The screen held the character **exactly one byte column left** of where the checker composited it. Capture 2
read `x=120` (col 35) from the slot record; the displayed buffer had last been drawn at `x=116` (col 34) —
which is where capture 1 found it and scored 0.

**The record holds the position the VM has DECIDED; the buffer holds the position it was DRAWN at**, and those
differ by one step until that buffer is redrawn.

Reading x/y from `ch_last` — per (character, slot), written when that slot last saved, and a position change
always takes the peel path, so it *is* where that buffer drew — gives:

| variant | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| A cel fixed, pos fixed | 0/0 | 0/0 |
| B cel fixed, pos every step | 0/139 | **0/0** |
| C cel every step, pos fixed | 0/0 | 0/0 |
| D both every step | 0/144 | **0/0** |
| E both, no overlap | 222/0 | **0/0** |

**A cheaper diagnostic existed the whole time and I reached for it fourth.** Counts tell you *that* something
is wrong; coordinates often tell you *what*. Worth doing before the second ablation, not after the third.

#### 3D — The dense demo and the cadence

Cel and position now change every step as a VM-driven data sequence, so the visible change rate *is* the step
rate. This is the sequence P3.27 had to revert; it is now byte-exact and stable, 512 KB and 128 KB, intro PASS.

Cadence measured off the **cel byte** (not a VM counter):

```
  2 frames : 239        the floor being met
  3 frames : 120
  4 frames : 240        the draw overrunning
  mean 3.00 frames/step against a 2.60 table (13/5 = 3,3,2,3,2)
```

Reported as a **consequence**, per the codified policy. The distribution *is* the policy in data — steps that
fit hit the floor at 2, steps that do not take 4, nothing clamps it. The +0.40 average is our draw cost,
exactly as the oracle's 3.9 was its own.

#### 3E — Why the pacing gate is still not gateable, and why that is now a content problem

Jay, twice: *"i cant tell pacing from that animation there needs to be more"* (P3.27), then *"the pacing is
still not seeable by eye. just not enough motion to judge."*

He is right both times, and densifying did not fix it because **density is not the same as motion**. The demo
alternates two poses and oscillates ±4 px on the spot: something changes every step, but there is no *gait* —
nothing whose rate the eye can read or compare against the oracle.

**What would carry the judgement is the vizier's real walk cycle** (`Vwalk`, cels 48-53, `chx` carrying him
across the room). That is not a demo tweak: P3.18 measured each walk cel as drawn at **two** sub-byte phases
(the cycle nets +10 px, and 10 ≡ 2 mod 4), so a faithful walk is 12 baked cels plus phase selection from
`x & 3` in the draw. That is piece E's content.

**A cheaper halfway option exists and is named rather than quietly taken:** cycle the walk cels' shapes while
stepping in multiples of 4 px — one phase per cel, byte-aligned, *not* faithful placement, but continuous
directional motion with a real gait, enough to judge cadence. It would have to be labelled a pacing rig, not
the walk.

**The engine is not what is blocking this gate.** Everything the walk needs is in place: the parity rule is
solved, the cel table already carries parity per cel, `cel_blit_prep` is proven across 24 cel/phase
combinations, and the draw is byte-exact at every density tested.

#### 3F — Two method failures, both of the same kind

**A probe that could not fail loudly.** My first single-buffer script reported 0/0 for the flip-ON control —
"the bug vanished". That contradicted the harness, so I re-ran the harness three times (0/139 each, perfectly
deterministic) and then found the cause: **my one-off script did not check `build.bat`'s output**, so a failed
build left the previous binary and I measured the sparse demo. The harness has that check; the throwaway did
not. **A tool that cannot distinguish "the change had no effect" from "the change was never built" reports
them identically** — and the convenient one is the one you want to believe.

**A stale constant inside a measuring tool.** `port_vm_cadence.lua` divided by "the demo holds a cel for 4
steps" — true when written, false the moment this dispatch densified the sequence, and it would have reported
0.75 frames/step with complete confidence. Removed. A stale constant in an instrument does not read as an
assumption; it reads as a measurement.
