### 1 — Summary

**The stagger works: worst frame 27,476 = 93%, with 2,197 cycles spare.** First route in this arc to bring the
worst case inside budget.

**But not by the mechanism the arithmetic suggested, and the difference is the whole finding.** The rough
sum said "halve the shift work". That is wrong: **double buffering means each hardware frame must still DRAW
both characters**, or a flip shows a missing one. So the stagger does not remove a draw — **it removes a
re-SHIFT**, because the character that did not advance has an unchanged cel at an unchanged x, and its shifted
bytes are therefore identical to last frame's.

**Which means the stagger DEPENDS on the cache, and they are not two ideas that compose.** Jay's stagger and
the amortisation idea are the same mechanism seen from two angles: reuse shifted bytes when nothing about a
character changed. The stagger's contribution is *arranging for only one character to change per frame.*

| route | worst frame | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| today — both shift in one frame | 33,821 | 114% | over |
| **amortisation alone** | **33,821** | **114%** | **over** |
| **stagger (requires the cache)** | **27,476** | **93%** | **fits** |
| both — the same mechanism | 27,476 | 93% | fits |

**Amortisation alone does not help the frame that fails**: on the step where both characters advance, its
first frame still pays both shifts. It helps the *second and third* frames of a step, which already fit.

**Peel correctness survives** (§3C) and **double buffering survives** (§3D) — but only under the reading above,
where the three passes stay inside one frame. **The other reading — splitting the passes across frames — does
reintroduce P3.32's interleave and must not be built.**

### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — Two readings of "stagger", and only one is safe

**Reading A — split the three passes across two frames** (erase+save in frame N, draw in frame N+1). **This
is P3.32's interleave by another route and it is unsafe.** The three passes exist so that no character's save
captures another's freshly-drawn pixels; separating them in time reintroduces exactly the window P3.32 closed,
and a buffer would be presented mid-sequence with characters erased but not redrawn.

**Reading B — advance one character per hardware frame; draw both, every frame, all three passes together.**
The VM steps the vizier in frame N and the princess in frame N+1. Each frame still runs erase-all →
save-all → draw-all over both characters. **Safe, and it is the one costed below.**

I am naming both because the dispatch's arithmetic ("each frame carries roughly half") describes Reading A,
and Reading A is the one that breaks.

#### 3B — The cost, from P3.39's measured figures

Shift cost per character, at **59 cy/row and 32 cy/byte**:

| | rows | drawn bytes | shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| vizier cel 67 | 48 | 156 | **7,824** |
| princess cel 12 | 43 | 119 | **6,345** |
| both in one frame | | | 14,169 → **33,821 = 114%** |

Staggered, each frame draws both but only one re-shifts:

| | baseline | shift | frame | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| frame A — vizier advances | 19,652 | 7,824 | **27,476** | **93%** |
| frame B — princess advances | 19,652 | 6,345 | 25,997 | 88% |

**The worst is 27,476, not the mean** — 2,197 cycles spare.

**Per-row overhead did not halve, and I did not halve it.** Each frame pays the full 59 cy × that character's
rows. The saving is entirely the *other* character's 6,345 or 7,824, not half of 14,169.

#### 3C — Peel correctness: survives, and here is why from buffer contents

Under Reading B, within frame A: erase both → save both → draw both. **The pass structure is untouched**; only
the *state* each character is drawn at differs (the vizier at his new cel, the princess at her previous one).

The question the dispatch asked — can one character's save capture the other's drawn pixels — is answered by
the pass order, not by whether anyone moved. **Saves all complete before any draw begins**, exactly as P3.32
arranged, so no save can see a freshly-drawn character. That holds regardless of which characters advanced.

**I am deliberately not resting this on "she hasn't moved."** That premise went false twice (cel changes,
overlap) and the dispatch was right to forbid it. The argument here is about *ordering within the frame*,
which is a property of `vm_frameadv`'s three `ch_pass` calls and does not depend on anyone's motion.

**One thing that does need checking before building:** `ch_scan`'s moved test is frame-wide — *if anything
moved, everybody peels*. Under a stagger something moves nearly every frame, so **the peel-skip would
effectively never fire.** That is not a correctness problem, and the costing above already assumes a full peel
every frame (it is the 19,652 baseline). But it means the stagger forfeits whatever the skip was saving on
quiet frames, which I have not quantified.

#### 3D — Double buffering: survives, and it is what forces the design

**Each buffer must contain both characters when presented.** `char_draw.s` keeps four peel slots, two per
character, and saves *into the buffer being drawn, not into its twin* — so each buffer's peel state is already
independent.

Under Reading B both characters are drawn into every buffer every frame, so **each buffer is complete and
correct at presentation**, and no flip can show a stale or mismatched pair.

**This is precisely why the saving is a re-shift and not a draw** (§1). Had we tried to skip drawing the
un-advanced character, its buffer would be missing her.

**The per-buffer last-drawn position stays out of the character record**, as it is today — the stagger does
not require it, because the un-advanced character's position is unchanged in both buffers.

#### 3E — The cache, and what it costs

The un-advanced character's shifted bytes are reused, so they must be held: **one shifted image per
character**, at `rows × (width + 1)`:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| vizier cel 67 | 48 × 8 | 384 B |
| princess cel 12 | 43 × 7 | 301 B |
| **total** | | **685 B** against **4,394 spare** |

Comfortable. **Validity bookkeeping is one flag per character** — invalidate when the cel or x changes, which
is exactly what `ch_moved` already computes per character in `ch_scan`. **The state the stagger needs is
state the engine already derives.**

### 5 — The routes, and what the stagger costs in fidelity

**Fidelity cost: one character updates one hardware frame late — 16.7 ms — in one step of the scene.** The
worst-case step is one of thirteen in `play 13` (P3.38b), and staggering costs nothing in the other twelve
because only one character advances in them anyway.

Against the alternatives already on the table:

- **Shorter cels** — a permanent change to the art, affecting every frame of the scene.
- **Staged loading** — a hold the oracle does not have, at a seam in the music.
- **Stagger** — 16.7 ms of lateness on one character, once.

**On the measurements in hand the stagger is the cheapest of the three by a wide margin.** That is a
comparison, not a recommendation; authorising it is Jay's.
