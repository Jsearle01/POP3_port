### 1 — Summary

**§0's lead is CONFIRMED, and it is worse than stated.** The full scene is 39,680 B against a
bank that is **31,744 B addressable, not 32,768** — each mapped pair loses its top 512 B to
MC3 and I/O. **Over by 7,936.**

**So a swap that only changes which two blocks are mapped cannot work.** Remapping is
necessary — nothing else reaches the upper half of the bank — but it is **not sufficient**.

**And every saving I can identify does not close it:**

```
full scene, as built                        39,680 B    over by 7,936
less all 7 mirrored variants (runtime mirror) 34,525    over by 2,781
less the table's facing dimension              33,845    over by 2,101
```

**~2,100 B must come from somewhere.** §4 names the three places it can come from and
recommends one. **Nothing was built.**

**★ The runtime mirror stops being optional.** It is not a 5,155 B saving; it is what makes
*pin-one-slot-and-rotate* viable at all (§3D). Without it `Pback`'s set does not fit a
rotating block.

### 3 — Reasoning

**3A — The bank is 31,744 B addressable, not 32,768, and the difference is not rounding.**
`gfx.s:178` sets `$FF90` with **MC3=1**, which holds `$FE00-$FEFF` as constant RAM, and
`$FF00-$FFFF` is always I/O. So whichever block sits at `$FFA7`, **its top 512 B cannot be
reached through the window**. Two mappings, two shadows: 32,768 − 1,024 = **31,744 B**.

The port already knows this for one mapping — 15,872 is that arithmetic — and it was never
carried to the two-mapping case. It is the same 512 B P3.67 §3B called *"exactly the thing
that would have been assumed away"*.

**3B — Only two blocks are remappable, and that is structural.** The draw window is
`$FFA4-$FFA7`. In 4-colour a framebuffer is 15,360 B and occupies `$8000-$BBFF` — i.e.
`$FFA4` entirely and most of `$FFA5`. **Those two cannot be repurposed while the scene
draws.** So the bank's four blocks are reached two at a time, through `$FFA6`/`$FFA7`, and
"map more of it" is not an available move.

**3C — The mirrored variants, verified rather than assumed** (Jay asked whether the princess's
early beats were included — they are):

```
p11_m_p1    590 B    her standing cel, mirrored
v48..v53_m  4,565 B  the walk cels, for Vexit's walk-out
            5,155 B  7 variants
```

Her eight turn cels are drawn **before** `Palert`'s `aboutface`, and `Pback` opens with a
**second `aboutface`** that flips her back, so cels 12-17 and `Pslump`'s are normal. She is
mirrored only for the held stretch between the two turns. Traced cel by cel, not inferred.

**3D — Pin-or-rotate: the numbers choose, and they choose only with the mirror.**

`$FFA6`'s block is 8,192 B fully usable; `$FFA7`'s is **7,680 B** (the shadow lands there).

- **Pin `$FFA6`** with the always-live set — the walk cels plus her standing cel, which
  `Vexit → Vwalk2` makes live at both ends. That set peaks at **6,769 B**, so it fits an 8,192 B
  block with 1,423 B spare. **This is the shape the content argues for**: the one thing that
  cannot be retired is exactly the thing a pinned slot holds.
- **Rotate `$FFA7`**, 7,680 B per beat. **As built, `Pback`'s increment is 9,538 B and does not
  fit.** With the runtime mirror its set is cels 12-17 with no mirrored copies — comfortably
  under. **So the mirror is not a saving to take later; it is a precondition for this shape.**

Rotating three blocks against one pinned gives 8,192 + 3 × 7,680 = **31,232 B** of schedulable
space against 34,525 B of content with the mirror. **Still 3,293 B over** — the rotation
schedule cannot invent capacity, it can only choose what is visible.

**3E — Pointer validity, which is the part that fails silently.** `walk_tab` holds absolute
`$Cxxx`/`$Dxxx` pointers. A pointer stays valid-looking while the wrong block sits under it and
the VM draws whatever bytes are there — **the capture-05 presentation exactly**. Three
mechanisms, and I recommend the second plus the third:

1. *Block-tagged pointers* — store (block, offset), remap per draw. Correct, but pays a remap
   inside `co_variant` on every cel and puts a window write on the hot path. **Rejected**: the
   thing that corrupted the bank last time was an unexpected MMU write, and this adds one per
   draw.
2. **Beat-scoped mapping** — the mapping changes **only at beat boundaries**, and the packer
   guarantees every cel a beat draws is in the pinned block or the block mapped for that beat.
   Pointers stay absolute and no draw-path code changes. **Recommended.**
3. **A draw-time assertion** — each cel record carries the block it needs; `co_variant`
   compares it against a `cur_block` byte the remap maintains and fails loudly on mismatch.
   **~10 cy per cel** (one `lda`/`cmpa`/`bne`), against 58,026 cy of budget. **Recommended, and
   it should SHIP, not be test-only** — the failure it catches is invisible by construction and
   took three dispatches to attribute the one time it happened.

**3F — Where the schedule lives.** In `bake_scene.PLAN`, beside the beats, emitted into the cel
image as a per-beat block number the room reads at each boundary. **One home per fact** (P3.31,
bitten three times here): a schedule that can drift from the content it serves is the worst
version of that bug, because the symptom is §3E's silent garbage. The bake already derives
phases, facings and now the mirror from the PLAN; the block assignment is the same derivation.

**3G — The cycle cost is not the problem.** A boundary remap is `pshs cc / orcc #$50 / lda
#blk / sta $FFA6 / inca / sta $FFA7 / puls cc` ≈ **30 cy**, once per beat, against 58,026 cy per
frame. The assertion is ~10 cy per cel drawn. **Neither is measurable.** The cost of this design
is entirely in RAM and in load time.

### 4 — The 2,100 B, and what has to give

**No arrangement of four blocks holds 34,525 B.** Three candidates:

1. **★ ONE MID-SCENE TRACK READ, HIDDEN IN A SONG HOLD — recommended, and it costs 1.2 s, not
   3.3.** P3.45 ruled out staged loading because *"PlayCut0 is continuous, nineteen play calls
   with no gap"*. **That is no longer true of the port:** P3.72l put the two measured song cues
   in as real beats — **761 frames (12.7 s) and 358 (6.0 s)** of deliberate stillness in which
   only the torches move. One track is 4,608 B and covers the shortfall twice over.

   **★★ THE 3.3 s/TRACK FIGURE THIS REPORT FIRST QUOTED IS THE PRE-DMK JVC NUMBER (P3.6) AND
   HAS NOT BEEN TRUE SINCE.** Measured on the current build (`harness/tools/load_timing.lua`,
   decomposing the room's three reads — 1 track, 1 track, 3 tracks):

   ```
   one TRACK     72 frames   1.20 s
   one SPIN-UP   36 frames   0.60 s
   3 spin-ups    1.80 s of the 9.27 s EXEC-to-room startup
   ```

   **★★★ AND THE SPIN-UP NEED NOT COST ANYTHING AT ALL.** `dr_spinup` is a *delay loop* —
   `ldx #$C000 / leax -1,x / bne`, 393,216 cycles of the CPU doing nothing — not a hardware
   wait. The motor reaches speed in physical time regardless of what the CPU executes. So the
   motor can be started, **the flame loop kept running for those 0.6 s**, and the read issued
   after. The spin-up is not merely avoidable by keeping the motor on; it is **hideable**.

   **So the honest cost of the mid-scene read is 1.2 s of frozen torches** — the transfer
   itself, which is polled with interrupts masked and has no DMA. That is still a visible
   deviation in a beat the oracle animates, and **it is Jay's call**; but it is a 1.2-second
   stutter inside a 12.7-second hold, not a 3.3-second one.
2. **Cut content.** ~2,100 B is about three of the vizier's cels. `Vraise` is 85 + 67×6 +
   68..75 + 83,84 — the six repeats of 67 cost nothing, but dropping intermediate frames of the
   raise would coarsen a gesture the oracle spends 18 plays on.
3. **Require 512 KB.** On a 512 KB machine the constraint vanishes entirely. The port has held
   a 128 KB floor since P3.10, and abandoning it is a product decision, not a technical one.

**I recommend (1)**, sequenced: the runtime mirror first (it is a precondition, §3D), then
pin-and-rotate with the assertion, then the single read for the remainder.

### 4B — the motor question, retracted and then re-decided (2026-08-11)

**I claimed a 1.2 s POP-local saving from keeping the motor running. It does not exist.**
`jsr dr_spinup` is unconditional at the head of BOTH public entries:

```asm
disk_read_range:
        lda     #DSK_POS
        sta     DSKREG
        jsr     dr_spinup        <- runs whether or not the motor is already turning
```

The 0.6 s is not the motor reaching speed; it is a delay loop burning 393,216 cycles. Leaving
`DSKREG` set changes nothing. **The measurement was already on file and I did not read it:**
load 2 ran moments after load 1, drive still warm, and still took the full 108 frames. I
inferred the saving from `load_tracks` clearing `DSKREG` and never checked what the driver does
on the way back in.

**Two real options were then costed:**

- **A′ — merge the two adjacent reads** (tracks 29-30) into one call, saving one spin-up. The
  naive form moves `FLAME_LOAD` from `$5800` to `$4200` and is **a fuse, not a margin**: the
  bundle expands to `$3000..$4193`, leaving **108 B** of in-place slack on a blob that grew
  from 4,286 to 4,500 B in the last few dispatches. The safe form keeps `FLAME_LOAD` and copies
  the 2,419 packed bytes up after the read — ~0.008 s against 0.60 s saved. **POP-local, no
  back-port, startup only.**
- **B — make the spin-up conditional in the HAL**, with a `dr_motor_on` flag and an exported
  `disk_read_motor_off` for callers to release the drive through. **0.6 s per read after the
  first**, and the only option that touches the mid-scene freeze (1.7 → 1.13 s).

**Jay checked B was purely additive, and it is** — one new export, cold path byte-identical,
nothing removed. The invariant it introduces ("nobody clears `DSKREG` behind the HAL's back")
can only be violated by a direct writer, and there are none in Karateka: `POP` has two
(`cutscene_room.s:937`, `intro_seq.s:321`), Karateka has **zero**. Karateka never releases the
drive, so it pays a full spin-up today for a motor that never stopped — **it inherits the
improvement rather than absorbing a cost.**

**★★★ HARD-STOP #1 FIRED ON THE BUILD.** `CLAUDE.md:200` — *"Karateka's repo is READ-ONLY"*;
`:212` — *"never modify Karateka"*; `:215` — *"Back-ports are separate explicit tasks … never an
automatic sync."* Against `hal_sync_check`, which **blocks the POP build on any HAL drift**.
Changing POP's `disk_read.s` without Karateka does not merely fail to land the feature — **POP
stops building at all.** Reported rather than built.

**AND THE STRATEGIC QUESTION CHANGED THE ANSWER.** Asked which option serves the full game and
the HAL's eventual life as an OS kernel, I had been optimising for the cutscene. From that
footing A′ is wrong: it pushes driver knowledge into the caller ("merge your adjacent reads
because the driver is wasteful"), which every future caller then inherits and the ones that
cannot merge simply pay. A cutscene reads three times; a game reads constantly.

**★ AND THE SPIN-UP IS NOT THE ONLY UNKEPT STATE.** `disk_read_range` also issues an
**unconditional `FDC_RESTORE`** — a full seek to track 0 — before seeking to the target, on
every call. Reading track 30 steps all the way in and back out. The driver does not remember
where its head is for the same reason it does not remember whether its motor is turning: **it
holds no device state at all.** One defect, two symptoms, and `dr_motor_on` is the first byte
of the structure that fixes both.

**Recommendation, revised: take B, as a deliberate HAL task with its own gate**, scoped as *"the
disk driver tracks its device state"* rather than *"skip a delay"*, so the restore is examined
in the same pass. Not smuggled in behind a cutscene optimisation. **The §2G mechanics are Jay's
to choose** — POP-changed-and-mirrored, or an explicit one-time authorisation across both.

**Nothing was built.** The restore's cost has NOT been measured separately; it sits inside the
1.13-1.20 s/track figure and should be isolated before anyone sizes the work.
