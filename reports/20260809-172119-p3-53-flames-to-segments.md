### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — Where my 675 B came from, and why it was the wrong quantity

P3.52 §3E ran `cel_blit_prep.py` on `flame1/converted.s` and reported **75 segment bytes, replay OK**. The
figure is correct. **What it measures is the existing cel at phase 0** — byte-aligned, 2 bytes wide, which is
the placement that puts torch 0 at 112 and torch 1 at 200.

**The alignment fix is precisely the thing that invalidates it.** A torch at a true position sits at a non-zero
sub-byte phase, an 8 px cel starting mid-byte spans **3** byte columns, and the segment stream grows with the
footprint — measured at **91-93 B** for flame1 against 75. Two sets, wider cels: **1,741 B**, not 675.

**I quoted a measurement of the unfixed state as the cost of the fixed one**, and the dispatch built its
headline on it. The number was real; the thing it described was not the thing being decided. *(Same family as
P3.52's own finding — a constant that is genuinely in the source, for a configuration you are not in.)*

#### 3B — The peel geometry, which neither of us costed

The dispatch's §1 says *"the peel becomes the shared `blit_save`/`blit_erase`... which need no per-cel code"*,
and that is true. **What it does not say is that the footprint changes.** `PEEL_BYTES equ 26 ; 13 rows x 2
bytes` is sized to the current 2-byte cel; a 3-byte cel needs **39**, and `peel_base rmb PEEL_BYTES*4` grows
from 104 to 156 B.

That is **+52 B of RAM and 50% more bytes moved by every save and every erase, every frame** — on top of
swapping hand-optimised straight-line code for generic primitives. The cycle question the dispatch rightly
insists on measuring is therefore **larger than it looks**, because the work grows as well as the cost per unit
of work.

**This is the P3.48 lesson in a new place:** a representation change that looks like a swap of equivalent parts
moves state geometry with it, and the peel is where this project's accumulating bugs live.

#### 3C — The conflict, stated rather than resolved

The dispatch is explicit: **both torches to their true positions.** Jay is explicit, twice, and the second time
after *looking at the result*: **leave the left one alone.**

Both positions are defensible. The oracle's arithmetic says torch 0 belongs at 111. Jay looked at 113 and
rejected it, and asked for 112 back — a position that is 1 px from true and is what the port has always shown.
**"Faithful to the oracle" and "what Jay accepts on the screen" are different standards**, and CLAUDE.md §2
ranks Jay above the source for exactly this kind of call.

**Option B is also the cheaper one** — 532 B freed against 370 — because torch 0 keeps its 2-byte phase-0 cel
and only torch 1 needs the wider set. So the instruction and the economics agree with each other and disagree
with the dispatch.

**I have not chosen.** Building either would spend a large change on a guess about which standard applies.

#### 3D — Why I stopped here rather than carrying through

The dispatch says carry through and stop only for something new. **Three things are new**, and each one moves
the decision:

1. The space payoff is **370-532 B**, not 1,488 — a quarter of what justified the change.
2. The change is **larger than scoped**: it moves `PEEL_BYTES`, `peel_base`, and the per-frame byte count of
   both peel primitives. The dispatch costs none of that.
3. The target placement is **contested between the dispatch and Jay**.

Against that: this session has run **P3.42 through P3.53**, and the change touches the peel — the subsystem
with four accumulating bugs, every one written late in a long session. **HARD-STOP #5.** The measurements that
make the decision are done and in this report; the build itself is a fresh-session task with an unambiguous
target.

**The conversion is still right.** It retires the last compiled sprites, it fixes the alignment, and it frees
space rather than costing it. It is simply worth doing against the correct numbers and the intended target.
