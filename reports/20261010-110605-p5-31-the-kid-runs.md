### 1 — Summary

**The kid runs.** The probe draws LEVEL0 screen 1, then drives the kid with the oracle's own `startrun`
sequence across the top floor. Each step:
1. the sequencer interprets the sequence bytes;
2. the per-page peel restores what that buffer last covered;
3. the save under the new frame;
4. the transform draws him;
5. the foreground pass redraws the post over him;
6. a VBL-synced flip.

**He starts where the demo's kid starts, from the oracle's own trace** (CharX 191, CharY 55, facing
left, behind screen 1's post). **His first 11 positions equal the trace's.** The sequence was checked
two ways: a hand transcription and `seq_graph.py`'s parse of SEQTABLE.S agree for 80 steps.

**Correctness:** five captured steps (6, 11, 14, 24, 26) are each **15,360/15,360** against a
prediction with no history. Any peel residue would show; none did, after **two bugs this check
caught first** (§3E).

**Cost:** **42.4K cycles per step** on average (sequencer 0.3K, peel restore 8.5K, save 9.0K, draw
18.0K, foreground 6.6K). That is **24.6% of the oracle's own time for the frames walked** (max 35.3%).

**Cadence:** **9.96 fps measured, against the oracle's 9.65 over its run frames.**

**Three defects found and fixed in the gameplay path's own code:**
- the swap unmapping the tile page;
- **`xf_blit` breaking the HAL's DP=0 interrupt invariant**;
- my pacing off by one.

**And one latent bug found in shipped code, NOT fixed:** `lz_unpack` copies a count of 256·k as
256·(k+1). Every shipped asset is unaffected (measured); prod would move if it were fixed (§3F).

#### 3A — The sequence and the interpreter (AC2, AC3, AC4, AC11)

*Authority: source (SEQTABLE.S, COLL.S, CTRLSUBS.S) for the semantics; **the oracle's trace** for the
positions.*

- **The sequence is `startrun`** (seqtab entry 1, SEQTABLE.S:159-177). POP has no sequence called
  "walk": locomotion is the run, and the careful steps end in `goto stand`.
  - `act,1`;
  - frames 1–6, with `chx` 8/3/3 after frames 4–6;
  - then `runcyc1..8`, frames 7–14 with `chx` 5/1/2/4/5/2/3/4 and `tap,1` before frames 9 and 13;
  - `goto runcyc1`.

  It is read through `seq_graph.parse_seqtable()` (operand counts from ANIMCHAR's own dispatch).
- **AC3 — implemented:** frame bytes, `goto`, `chx`, `act` (operand consumed, CharAction not
  modelled), `tap` (operand consumed, the footstep **sound not played**).
  **Not implemented:** `aboutface`, `up`, `down`, `chy`, `setfall`, `ifwtless`, `die`, `jaru`, `jard`,
  `effect`, `nextlevel`. Any of them halts the walk with `wk_err` = the opcode. Semantics as ANIMCHAR
  [COLL.S:994-1128] and the cutscene VM [char_draw.s:1574-1589]: opcodes precede the frame they
  affect, and a `goto` costs no step.
- **AC4 — `Fdx` and `chx`, reconciled.** Both go through `ADDCHARX` [CTRLSUBS.S:353-363], which
  NEGATES its argument facing left:
  - **`chx`** sets CharX := ADDCHARX(delta) [COLL.S:999-1005] and moves the kid **permanently**;
  - **`Fdx`** offsets one frame's **draw** only: FCharX = 2·(ADDCHARX(Fdx) − 58) + parity
    [CTRLSUBS.S:807-839].

  So facing left, the image's left edge is Apple px 2·(CharX − Fdx − 58) + PL, port px +20, and its
  bottom row is CharY + Fdy. Colour: swap = PL (P5.29).
- **AC11 — positions checked against the ORACLE'S TRACE**, the cheap case: P5.21's
  `oracle_step_trace.txt` logs the demo kid's record at every game frame.

  - Frames 1..11 are at CharX 191, 191, 191, 191, 183, 180, 177, 172, 171, 169, 165. That is

#### 3B — The peel, per page, and the order against DRAWALL (AC5)

*Authority: source (GRAFIX.S:485-505, §5.313's SNGPEEL, char_draw.s:107).*

**Two peel buffers, one per page,** each with its own saved offset, rows and width.
- Each step restores what **this buffer** saved two steps ago (`ldb HAL_gfx_cur_back` indexes every
  peel array), then saves under the new frame.
- The buffers sit in the page's staging area (`$3000`), which is free once the page is unpacked.
  Runtime only.

**Order, against DRAWALL** (SNGPEEL → ZEROPEEL → DRAWWIPE → DRAWBACK → DRAWMID → DRAWFORE):

| oracle | here | divergence |
|---|---|---|
| SNGPEEL | `blit_erase_full` from this page's peel | none |
| ZEROPEEL | — | none needed: the peel is overwritten by the next save |
| DRAWWIPE / DRAWBACK | — | nothing in the background changes on this screen |
| DRAWMID (save underlayers & draw) | `blit_save_full`, then `xf_blit` | none |
| DRAWFORE | `fore_draw_rect`, culled to the kid's rectangle | **culled** — see §3C |

#### 3D — ★ Two defects in the gameplay path, found by the byte checks and the cadence measurement

1. **The swap unmaps the tile page.** `HAL_gfx_swap` ends in `gfx_map_blocks`, which writes all four
   window registers `$FFA4–$FFA7` [gfx.s:747-756]. After the first flip, `$C000` was a framebuffer
   block, and `fore_draw_rect` read its list from that garbage.
   - **Steps 11, 14 and 24 came out exact by luck**: the kid touched no fore piece.
   - **Steps 6 and 26 differed by 49 and 97 bytes**, all on the post. The kid was visible in front of
     it.
   - Fix: re-map after every flip, as `cutscene_room.s`'s `cel_bank_map` does. Both steps are now
     EXACT.
2. **★ `xf_blit` broke the HAL's DP=0 interrupt invariant.**
   - The VBL handler increments its frame counter through the direct page and documents *"DP=0
     invariant holds"* [irq_vbl.s:21-22, 79]. `xf_blit` runs with DP = its own page, and its per-row
     IRQ window (P4.29) opened with DP still set.
   - A VBL landing there incremented `XF_DPPAGE·256 + $11`, so **the tick was lost** and a byte outside
     the routine's 14 was written.
   - **Found by the cadence measurement:** steps of 5–8 display frames against a constant request.
   - **Fix: `XF_WINDOW` sets DP = 0 for the window**, +16 cy per row.
   - Every case P5.29 measured passes again with it: **4,720/4,720 and 2,048/2,048**. P5.20's cycle
     figures for `xf_blit` are therefore ~16 cy/row low.
3. **My pacing was one frame long.** I waited `WK_SPEED` frames and then swapped, and the swap waits
   for one more VBL. It now waits `WK_SPEED − 1`.

#### 3F — ★ A latent bug in the shipped `lz_unpack` (found, measured, NOT fixed)

I first shipped `xf_blit`'s tables packed (7,424 → 2,355 B) and expanded them with `lz_unpack`. The 6809
ran its writer past the output into its own blob at `$6B00` and then ran away into the disk driver,
traced frame by frame.
- **The cause:** both copy loops keep a count as B (low byte) + `lz_cnt` (high byte). When the low
  byte is 0 and the high byte ≥ 1, the first pass already copies 256 (B wraps) and `lz_cnt` adds 256
  per unit, **so a count of 256 copies 512**. The tables' zero runs and 256-byte incompressible
  stretches produce exactly that.
- **The packer cannot avoid it in general:** literal runs cannot be split without a match.
- **`lz_pack.decompress_6809`** now models the routine, bug included. **Every compressed asset the
  port ships decodes EXACT under it** (`tile_page`, `intro_screen`, `prolog1/2`, `intro_bundle`,
  `intro_prog`, `scene_prog`, `flames`, `msys_player`, `cel_pg0–3`, `cel_res`). **The bug is latent.**
- **Not fixed:** `lz_unpack` is linked into the prod binaries. The tables now link raw (§3G).
