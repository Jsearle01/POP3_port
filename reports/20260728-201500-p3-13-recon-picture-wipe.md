### 1 — Summary

Jay saw the oracle reveal the prologue text screen with a horizontal left-to-right
swipe confined to the picture area. It is real, it is in the source, and **it is not
an effect** — `DblExpand` *is* the wipe. The routine `DBLEXPAND` jumps immediately to
one named `WipeRgtExp`, which walks 80 columns left to right expanding each straight
into the page being displayed. The sweep is the decompression order made visible.

Our port does the opposite in every respect: it decodes into the hidden buffer and
page-flips, so the picture arrives complete in one frame. **P3.8 already knew** — the
beat table comment says "the oracle spends these wiping the picture in over the
splash" — and modelled the wipe's *duration* as a plain pause.

This affects **three of the six beats** (splash, prolog1, prolog2) and the reprise.

The task is not hard to describe and is awkward to build, for one reason: the oracle
composites over the image it is displaying, and our double-buffer design has no way
to write the buffer that is on screen. §3 sets out three ways round that with their
real costs; the choice is Jay's, because they differ in RAM, in fidelity, and in
whether 128 KB keeps working.

### 3 — Reasoning

**The oracle, verified in source.** `MASTER.S:846`:

```asm
Prolog1  lda #pacProlog
         sta RAMRDaux
         jsr DblExpand        ; <- no page flip anywhere
```

`UNPACK.S:246`:

```asm
DBLEXPAND  sta CrnDatPtr+1
           lda #1
           sta CrnDatPtr
           jmp WipeRgtExp

WipeRgtExp lda #0
           sta XClmPos
:Loop      lda #0 / sta YScrPos / jsr ExpandClm   ; column, top to bottom
           lda #1 / sta YScrPos / jsr ExpandClm
           inc XClmPos
           lda XClmPos
           cmp #80
           bne :Loop
```

80 columns, left to right, each expanded top-to-bottom into the visible page. There
is no separate wipe routine and no timing loop — it takes exactly as long as
decompressing takes, which is the ~101 frames our beat table already budgets.
[Authority: source. Confirmed against Jay's live observation, which is what prompted
the search.]

**Scope.** `DblExpand` serves `unpacksplash`, `Prolog1`, `Prolog2` and the epilog, so
**every whole-picture beat wipes in**. Captions do not: they go through
`DeltaExpPop` onto the hidden page and flip, which P3.3's trace independently
confirmed (the screen checksum does not move across the four frames of drawing). Our
port already matches the captions exactly. `DELTAEXPWIPE` exists as a vector and is
never called in this tree.

**What the port does instead.** `sq_beat` runs `jsr load_screen` (disk read +
`lz_unpack` into the back buffer), then `jsr HAL_gfx_swap`, then
`hold_frames(beat_pre)`. Right duration, wrong appearance: 101 frames of the finished
picture rather than 101 frames of it arriving.

**Jay's "the border is left untouched", measured.** Comparing the splash to prolog1,
the bytes that differ are **columns 10–148, rows 0–169** of the 160×192 framebuffer.
Columns 0–9 and 149–159 never change — they are the +20 px centring margins of the
virtual-resolution contract, which have no counterpart on the Apple II, where 80
columns *is* the whole active width and everything outside is physical screen border.
So the observation gives a clean rule: **the sweep covers the centred 140-byte content
region and must leave the margins alone.**

---

**THE HARD PART: the oracle writes the page it is displaying, and we cannot.**

The oracle has one page and destroys it in place; its source is the small compressed
stream. We have two buffers, and the HAL maps only the *back* one into the draw
window at `$8000`. `gfx_map_blocks` is HAL-private. So a wipe needs either a way to
write the front buffer, or a way to hold two full images plus a composite.

Three options, all real:

**(A) Decode straight to the visible page, column-ordered.** The faithful one — it
*is* what the oracle does. Requires storing these three images **transposed**
(column-major) so sequential decoding writes column 0 top-to-bottom, then column 1.
Cost: `lz_unpack`'s output pointer becomes strided (+160 per byte, wrap to the next
column), and — the killer — **its match copy reads back from its own output**, so
back-references must be resolved in stream order too. That destroys the
`lda ,y+ / sta ,x+` inner loop that makes the decoder fast, and the decoder is
currently ~680 ms per screen. No extra RAM. Highest fidelity, worst risk.

**(B) Decode as now, then blit column-by-column into the front buffer**, paging the
front buffer's four blocks through `$FFA2` the way `bank_copy` already does. Cost: a
column is 192 bytes at stride 160 spanning all four blocks, and block boundaries fall
mid-row (8192/160 = 51.2 rows), so each column step needs up to four MMU remaps and
careful boundary handling. No extra RAM, works at 128 KB, moderate fiddliness.

**(C) Composite in the back buffer and flip every frame.** Only ever writes the
mapped buffer — no MMU tricks beyond what `bank_copy` does today. Needs the incoming
picture to live somewhere readable while the composite is built, i.e. **a second
32 KB bank**. Free at 512 KB (48 blocks spare); **impossible at 128 KB**, where the
machine is exactly full and the only spare bank holds the splash. Note the
double-buffer subtlety: after each flip the buffer you are writing is two frames
stale, so each frame must copy the columns added over the last *two* frames, not one.
Simplest code, costs 128 KB support.

My recommendation is **(B)**: it keeps 128 KB working, needs no asset change, and its
difficulty is bounded fiddliness rather than a rewrite of the decoder's hot loop. But
(C) is materially less code if Jay is willing to make the wipe a 512 KB-only
enhancement, and that is a product decision, not a technical one.

---

**Interaction with prefetch — they conflict, and this is the important part.**

The prefetch plan assumes a beat's picture appears via `HAL_gfx_swap`, with the read
paid under the *previous* beat's hold. A wipe means the picture does **not** appear
by swap; it is painted over ~101 frames during the beat's own PRE.

They are still compatible, but the ordering changes: prefetch must deliver the
decoded picture *into the place the wipe reads from* (the back buffer for (B), the
second bank for (C)) before the beat starts, and the wipe then runs during PRE with
no disk access at all. That is in fact **closer to the oracle**, which batches every
load up front and then runs the whole sequence untouched.

Doing the wipe first is therefore the better order: it fixes the destination
question, and prefetch then becomes "put the bytes there earlier".

**Pacing, and one number that is missing.** Beat PREs are 99 (splash), 101 (prolog1),
**0 (prolog2)** and 178 (reprise). Prolog2 has no PRE because the oracle's gap there
is the `PrincessScene` cutscene we have not built — so **there is no oracle-derived
duration for prolog2's wipe** and one will have to be chosen or traced. That is a
question for the Orchestrator, not something to invent here.

### 4 — Verification (AC-by-AC) — proposed, for the build task

- **AC1** — the sweep is left to right, one pass, over columns 10–149 only; columns
  0–9 and 149–159 are byte-identical throughout.
- **AC2** — at completion the framebuffer is byte-identical to the current
  `prolog1.raw` / `prolog2.raw` / `intro_screen.raw`. The existing eleven byte
  comparisons must still pass unchanged — the wipe changes *how* the picture arrives,
  never what it is.
- **AC3** — the sweep completes within the beat's PRE, so beat timings are unchanged
  against the oracle's frame numbers.
- **AC4** — captions still appear and vanish in a single frame (they use the flip
  path and must not regress).
- **AC5** — 128 KB still passes, or the task explicitly records that the wipe is
  512 KB-only and what 128 KB does instead.
- **AC6** — a mid-wipe capture. The current harness only proves the *endpoint*; a
  capture partway through the sweep is what would actually prove a wipe happened.
  Without it this task can be "verified" by a test that a plain swap also passes.

**AC6 is the one that matters**, and its absence is why this defect survived from
P3.8 to now — see §7.

### 7 — Uncertainty flags

- **Our whole test suite passes with the wipe entirely absent, and would keep
  passing.** It checks framebuffers at settled capture points and beat timings against
  the oracle's frame numbers. A missing 101-frame transition changes neither: the
  endpoint is identical and the duration was reproduced as a pause. **Matching the
  duration of a behaviour we did not implement is what made the gap invisible.** This
  is the second time in three tasks that a green suite has meant less than it read as.
- **The (A)/(B)/(C) costs are reasoned from the code, not measured.** I have not
  prototyped the strided decoder or the four-block column blit, so the difficulty
  ordering is an estimate. (B)'s boundary handling in particular could be worse than
  it looks.
- **No duration exists for prolog2's wipe** (PRE 0), as above.
- **Whether the reprise wipes is inferred**, not traced: `SilentTitle` calls
  `unpacksplash`, which is `DblExpand`, so it should. Not confirmed against a run.
- **I have not checked whether the wipe's speed is uniform.** `WipeRgtExp` has no
  delay — each column takes as long as its data takes to expand, so a column of flat
  colour is faster than a detailed one. A constant-rate sweep may look subtly
  different from the oracle's. Worth a trace before choosing a pacing model.
