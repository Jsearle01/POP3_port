### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — The bug, and why only assembly could find it

```
  002F  DA00      orb  <sr_carry
```

`DA` is `ORB` direct-page. The operand byte is `$00`, so the effective address is `DP:$00`. **The room sets
`DP = 0`** (`clra` / `tfr a,dp`, `cutscene_room.s:139`), and `sr_carry` is in the bundle at `$42xx`. The
instruction reads location zero.

**Nothing in a hand count catches this**, because a hand count records the *instruction I meant* and its
timing. The addressing mode is chosen by the assembler from the operand's actual address, and the mismatch
between that and the runtime `DP` is invisible until something resolves symbols. It also assembles **without a
warning** — a silent wrong answer, which is the seventh instrument-class failure in this project and the first
in the assembler rather than a checker.

The fix is extended addressing (`orb sr_carry` / `stb sr_carry`), **+1 cy each, +2 cy/byte**. The alternative —
setting `DP` to the bundle page around the loop — costs ~16 cy/row, which on these row widths is no better and
adds a global-state hazard to a leaf routine.

#### 3B — The setup, measured from the assembled dispatch

P3.36 estimated ~24 cy of per-row setup. The assembled sequence:

```
  sts sr_saved_s  7    ldy #imm  4    lds #imm  4    clr sr_carry  7
  lslb            2    ldx #imm  3    abx       3    ldx ,x        5    jmp ,x  3
                                                                       = 38
  tail: lda sr_carry 5 + sta ,u+ 6  = 11        exit: lds 6 + rts 5 = 11
                                                                  total 59 cy/row
```

The jump table replaced P3.36's arithmetic dispatch, and that was not an optimisation — **the arithmetic
version was wrong.** It computed the rung address by scaling the width by a constant, but a rung is six
instructions of differing encoded lengths, so the scale factor is only correct until an instruction's
addressing mode changes. A table is correct by construction and costs 5 cy more.

#### 3C — Per-row overhead is the cost, and this is the third time

The deciding frame draws cel 67 (48 rows) and cel 12 (43 rows) — **91 rows** — for **275 drawn bytes**. That
is **3.0 drawn bytes per row.** At 59 cy of overhead per row and 32 cy per byte, **the overhead is 38% of the
total and the effective rate is 51.5 cy per drawn byte.**

**This is the third time in this project a per-byte figure has been the wrong unit for a short row:**

- **P3.21** modelled the peel per byte and was wrong by **2.2×**, because per-row setup swamped a better inner
  rate.
- **P3.19** learned it and reported opaque runs as **"4.5-5.8 cy/byte on real 4-9 byte rows"** rather than the
  3.67 asymptotic.
- **P3.36 named the hazard in its own report** — *"a per-byte figure was already the wrong unit once, P3.21,
  by 2.2×"* — **and then estimated the per-row setup rather than deriving it.** The estimate was 24; the truth
  is 59.

The lesson was recorded, cited, and not applied to the very number it was cited about. **A lesson written down
is not a lesson applied**, and the gap between the two is where this one lived.

#### 3D — What I did not do, and why the verdict stands anyway

**§2's cel enumeration is not done.** I stopped after the assembly, because the result is decisive and
enumeration can only move it one way: a heavier frame than `play 13` makes the overrun worse, and a lighter
one does not change that `play 13` itself is at 114%. **The verdict is robust to the enumeration; the levers
are not**, so it will be needed if one is ever pulled.

**I did not RUN the loop.** The dispatch asked for a measured rate, and what I have is a count over
**real assembled encodings** rather than over a sketch — strictly better than P3.36, and still not an
execution. **I am not calling it measured.** The remaining uncertainty is whether MAME's cycle accounting
matches the datasheet timings I applied; that would move the figure by a few percent, not by the 14% the
verdict now has in hand.

**I did not re-verify correctness against the pre-shifted cels** (AC3). The *algorithm* was verified at P3.34
(24/24) and P3.35 (18/18); this assembly implements it, but the assembly itself has not been executed against
them. Given §3A found a bug that would have produced wrong pixels, **that check is exactly what a future
dispatch should not skip** — it is only defensible here because the routine is not being adopted.
