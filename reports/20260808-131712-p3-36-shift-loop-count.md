### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — The complete loop, and what "complete" added

P3.35's floor counted four instructions and omitted three things. Here they are, with the omissions marked:

```
  lda ,x+          6    the source byte
  ldb a,y          5    SHR[src] — the in-byte part
  orb <sr_carry    4    the previous byte's spill
  stb ,u+          6    the shifted byte
  ldb a,s          5    SHL[src] — this byte's spill        <- omitted from the floor
  stb <sr_carry    4    carried into the next iteration     <- omitted from the floor
                  30 cy/byte unrolled
  dec <sr_cnt      6                                        <- omitted from the floor
  bne              3
                  39 cy/byte rolled
```

**The floor was 21 and the truth is 30** — the omissions were 43% of the cost, which is why P3.35's "99% at a
floor" was correctly read as a failure rather than a pass.

**Per-row setup is ~24 cy** (park S, load both table bases, clear the carry, dispatch into the unrolled
ladder). On P3.19's real 4-9 byte rows that is 32.7-36.0 cy/byte — **~34 all-in on a typical 6-byte row.**
Reporting the all-in figure matters: P3.21 was wrong by 2.2× for exactly this reason, having used a per-byte
rate where per-row overhead dominated a short row.

#### 3B — The three optimisations taken, and why each is legitimate

Stated because a badly-written loop would wrongly kill the last survivors — P3.19's "infeasible" ruling was
measured against a per-byte `LSR`/`ROR` strawman, and this dispatch must not produce another.

**Tables, not shifts.** An in-register shift costs 8 cy at phase 1 and **24 at phase 3**, and must produce
*both* halves. Two table lookups cost 10 cy flat at every phase. Tables win from phase 1 up and the margin
grows with the phase.

**A permuted table, because accumulator-offset indexing is SIGNED.** `ldb a,y` reads Y+A with A as a *signed*
byte, so a plain 256-entry table indexes backwards for every value above 127 — a correctness trap, not a
performance one. Emitting the table rotated by 128 (entries `$80..$FF` first) and biasing Y by +128 makes all
256 values correct at the same 5 cycles, and costs nothing at run time.

**S as a fourth pointer.** The loop needs four simultaneous pointers — source, destination, SHR base, SHL base
— and the 6809 has three index registers. S carries the SHL base with interrupts masked, exactly as
`blit_blast` already borrows S for its destination.

**And the one that cannot be removed: there is no register-to-register OR on the 6809.** The spill from the
previous byte must travel through memory — `orb <sr_carry` / `stb <sr_carry` — costing **8 cy per byte** that
no arrangement of registers avoids. It is the same constraint that makes the merge path a 22 cy/byte floor
(P3.19). **That 8 cy is 27% of the loop**, and it is the reason the honest number lands where it does.

**I did not tune toward 28.4.** These three are optimisations I would take on their own merits. I know of no
further one that closes a 17% gap, and inventing one to reach a target would decide nothing — HARD-STOP #3.

#### 3C — What is verified, and what is not

**The algorithm is verified.** A byte-wise table shift with a carry reproduces `cel_blit_prep`'s pixel-space
bake exactly — **24/24 combinations at P3.34, 18/18 for spans at P3.35** — against a truth source that shares
nothing with it. This loop implements precisely that algorithm.

**The assembly encoding of it is not.** `shift_row.s` is a **counting artifact**: it elides seven of the nine
unrolled rungs (each identical, each the same 30 cycles) and has not been assembled or run. So the *count* is
of a real instruction sequence — P3.19's method — while the *file* is not yet shippable code.

I am flagging that rather than letting the commit imply otherwise, because HARD-STOP #2 forbids reporting a
rate for a loop I would not ship. **The loop's shape is one I would ship; the file is not yet that loop.**
Nothing in the verdict depends on the elided rungs — they are copies — but the distinction belongs in the
record.

#### 3D — Where this leaves the representation

| candidate | space | time |
|---|---|---|
| pre-shifted | **over by 8,658 B** | 66%, measured |
| shifted, (b) spans | fits | over — 99% at a floor that is 43% short (§3A) |
| shifted, (a)/(c) | fits | **over — 102% inner, 107% all-in** |

**No representation clears both budgets as the scene is currently drawn.** That is the answer this arc has
been converging on, and it is now measured rather than suspected.

**The hybrid does not rescue it**, and P3.35 §3E already said why: it pre-shifts single-phase cels and
runtime-shifts two-phase ones, but the heaviest frame is heaviest *because both characters are moving*, and
moving cels are the two-phase ones. In the frame that sets the budget the hybrid pays close to the full shift
cost. I did not cost it further, since the dispatch made that conditional on the loop leaving it live, and it
does not.

### 5 — Which lever the number points at — reported, not pulled

The gap is **~2,010 cy, about 17%** of the shift cost. Three levers exist, and the number points at one of
them first — but **not at a lever at all until one measurement is redone.**

**Do this first: re-measure the heaviest frame under the three-pass structure.** Every percentage in this
report inherits **19,579 from P3.19, measured before the three-pass frame existed.** The gap is 2,010 cy and
an inherited baseline is not obviously accurate to that. **It is cheap, and it could move the verdict in
either direction.** Pulling a lever before re-measuring would be acting on a number two dispatches old.

Then, if the gap survives:

- **Fewer bytes in the heaviest frame** — 178 → ~148 B/cel would close it. The heaviest frame draws two full
  characters; the oracle's own script may not require both at full size simultaneously. This is the lever the
  arithmetic points at most directly, because the cost is linear in bytes and the shortfall is modest.
- **More time** — the three-pass frame's structure. Related to the re-measurement above and possibly the same
  work.
- **More space** — banking, or **staged loading, which Jay deferred rather than closed.** The dispatch notes
  the `PlaySongI s_Buildup` sting between `Vstop` and `Vraise` as where a hold would most plausibly hide. The
  objection there is fidelity, not feasibility, and it is Jay's call.

**I have pulled none of these.**
