## Form B Report — P4.2 — what a tone costs

**Class:** design + one measurement. wip. **Nothing chosen, no sound system built.** The only
executable added is the instrument.

**★★★ THE NUMBER, MEASURED ON THE MACHINE. A GIME-timer FIRQ toggling the DAC costs
`+808 / +1,923 / +4,013` cycles per frame at ~468 / ~999 / ~2000 Hz — 3.6% / 7.3% / 14.3% of the
VBL budget. The oracle's own shape, a foreground bit-bang, costs `+24,656` — 83.4% of a frame for
one modest tone. Control passed; both runs agree to within 3 cycles.**

**★★★ AND THE FORK RESOLVES ITSELF ON THOSE NUMBERS: blocking is structurally impossible and
interrupt-driven is affordable. I am not calling that a choice — it is what the measurement says,
and §4's "do not choose" stands.**

### 3 — Reasoning

**3A — ★★★ THE MEASUREMENT (AC2).**

```
   phase  what                 frames  spins/f   work cyc/f   vs base   % of VBL
   0      baseline (no audio)  119     4229.1    256          +0        0.9%
   1      FIRQ ~468 Hz tone    120     4113.6    1064         +808      3.6%
   2      FIRQ ~999 Hz tone    120     3954.4    2178         +1923     7.3%
   3      FIRQ ~2000 Hz tone   120     3655.8    4268         +4013     14.3%
   4      BLOCKING bit-bang    600     706.8     24911        +24656    83.4%
```

**Method**, and it is this project's own established one [mame-idioms-coco3-port.md §0/§0a]:
MAME's Lua exposes no cycle counter on this build, so cost is read off the VBL spin —
`HAL_time_vbl_wait` burns 7 cycles per idle iteration, and `work = 29,859 − spins×7`. ★ **The
absolute rests on that 7; the DIFFERENCE between phases in one run does not, and every conclusion
here is a difference.**

**Cost per interrupt, derived from the three points:** 808 / (936 Hz ÷ 59.92) = **51.7 cyc**;
1,923 / (1,999 ÷ 59.92) = **57.6**; 4,013 / (4,000 ÷ 59.92) = **60.1**. ★★ **Consistent, and the
slight rise with rate is what a fixed per-interrupt cost looks like when the entry overhead stops
being amortised.** So an interrupt player's cost is `rate × ~55 cycles`, and that single figure
prices any tone.

★ **Liveness, which makes the timer numbers checkable rather than assumed:** the probe counted
**7,929 FIRQ entries over 120 frames = 66.1 per frame**, against 4,000 Hz ÷ 59.92 = **66.8
predicted**. The timer is running at the rate the design says.

**The control** is internal and free: phase 4 bit-bangs a known 300 toggles per frame, so it must
dominate. `+24,656 cycles` — it does, by 30×. ★ A run where it did not would mean the spin count
was not tracking work, and the script says so instead of printing numbers.

**3B — ★★★ WHAT THE NUMBERS DECIDE, AND WHAT THEY DO NOT.**

**Blocking is excluded, and not marginally.** 83.4% of one frame for a single sustained tone,
against a scene whose worst beat already spends 92.6%. There is no version of interleaving that
recovers 83 points. ★ **That is not a preference between architectures; it is one architecture
being unavailable.**

**Interrupt-driven is affordable — with a caveat I must state precisely, because the obvious
comparison is the wrong one.** P3.102 measured the exit walk at 63.184 ms of work against a
66.753 ms four-frame budget: **5.6% headroom, ≈1,596 cycles per frame.** A ~999 Hz FIRQ tone at
1,923 cyc/f would exceed that and push the iteration from 4 frames to 5 — the 8→10 step change on
a scene Jay has gated.

★★★ **BUT THE EXIT WALK HAS NO MUSIC, AND THAT MAKES 5.6% THE WRONG NUMBER TO PRICE AGAINST.**
In the oracle, `PlaySongI` calls `pburn`/`pstars`/`pflow` and **not** the character advance —
characters do not move while a song plays. The port mirrors that: the song beats are `hold_frames`
with torches only. **So music plays in the CHEAP frames, and the expensive walk frames are
silent.** ★ **I have not measured the port's headroom during a song hold** — P3.102 measured the
entry and exit walks, not the holds. **That is the missing number, and it is the one that prices
the design.** Named in §8, not guessed at here.

**3C — SYNTHESIS, NOT SAMPLES — CONFIRMED (AC3).**

`MSYS.S` carries a `NOTE` table of **75 period values** (122, 254, 238 … 9) and a `LENGTH` table
of durations; `SOUND.S`'s twenty effects reduce to `tone(pitch, duration)`. ★★ **There is no
sample content anywhere to recover** — the Apple's output is a one-bit speaker toggled by CPU
timing, so the entire audio signal is described by *frequency* and *duration*. A sample path would
be inventing information the source does not contain. **Confirmed, not overturned.**

★ **And a constraint that fell out of building the instrument and was not previously known:** the
GIME timer is **12 bits, ceiling 4095**, and at TINS=1 (279.365 ns) that puts the **lowest
reachable tone at ~468 Hz**. `NOTE`'s table runs to period 254 — whether the oracle's low notes
fall below 468 Hz is **not yet checked**, and if they do, the interrupt approach needs either
TINS=0 (63.695 µs, far too coarse) or a software divider. **Named, not solved.**

**3D — ★★ THE PRIOR ART, AND IT IS BETTER THAN P4.1 COULD SEE (AC4).**

P4.1 found Sock Master binary-only and Fiscarelli a disk image. **Glen Hewlett's work is readable
6809 assembly on GitHub**, and there is more of it than the dispatch named:

- **`Defender_CoCo3`**, **`Joust_CoCo3`**, **`ROBOTRON_CoCo3`**, **`Rick_Dangerous_CoCo_3`** —
  arcade transcodes, assembly, public.
- ★★★ **`MIDI-2-CoCo-Converter`** — *"Software to convert MIDI music files to be played on a CoCo
  **in the background of your own games**."* Assembly. **That is precisely POP's shape: background
  music under a running program.**
- ★★ **And its README names three target player formats: "Simon's dipole player", GMC, and PSG.**
  P4.1 recorded Jonassen as *"relevant but not readable — no published source in three
  searches."* **This converter targets his format**, which is a concrete route to it that P4.1
  did not have.

★ **What I did NOT get:** the README carries no cycle costs, voice counts or interrupt details —
those are in `PSGPLAYER.asm` / `GMC_Play.asm` / `Dipole_Part1.asm`, which I did not read.

**3F — §2H's THREE CHECKS.**

1. **A second mechanism for a different object class?** ★ Yes, and it changed the measurement's
   design: an interrupt player's cost is `rate × per-interrupt`, so **one tone frequency could
   not have answered it** — hence three phases and a curve. A single number would have been read
   as "the cost of audio" and been wrong for every other note.
2. **The calling routine.** The cost is not the handler's — it is the handler's **times the timer
   rate**, and the timer rate is set by the note. **The note data prices the design, not the
   player.**
3. **Grep the reports.** P3.102's 5.6% headroom is the figure everything wanted to be priced
   against, and §3B is why it is the wrong one: **it belongs to a beat that has no music.**

### 5 — The three things surfaced for Jay, not decided

1. **★★ TAIL SILENCE.** The port's beats are fixed lengths; a rendition shorter than its beat
   leaves **silence at the tail rather than a short beat**. The alternatives — pad, loop, stretch
   the tempo, or shorten the beat and move everything after it — are audible and visual in
   different proportions. **Yours.**
2. **★★ THE SCENERY-COUPLING TRAP.** The oracle clocks `pburn`/`pstars`/`pflow` **once per music
   slice**; the port's torches already run on their own `flm_cad 2,2,3`. ★ **That coupling is
   already not reproduced, and a timer-interrupt player must not re-introduce it** — the obvious
   implementation, advancing scenery from the audio interrupt, would do exactly that and would
   make the flicker rate a function of the note being played.
3. **★ THE LOADED SET, DEFERRED WITH A REASON.** The oracle swaps three music sets from disk into
   the same `$D000`, and a song id is meaningless without knowing which is resident. **The
   cutscene is entirely title-set**, so the port needs no concept of a set yet. **Recorded as a
   deferred decision rather than an unnoticed gap** — gameplay's music is set 2 and will force it.

### 8 — Uncertainty flags

- **★★★ THE HEADROOM DURING A SONG HOLD IS NOT MEASURED.** §3B. P3.102's 5.6% belongs to the exit
  walk, **which has no music**. Until the hold's headroom is measured, `+808/+1,923/+4,013` cannot
  be turned into "fits" or "does not fit". **This is the next measurement and it is small.**
- **★★ The ~468 Hz floor may bite.** The timer's 12-bit ceiling sets it; whether `NOTE`'s low
  periods go below it is **unchecked**.
- **★ Hewlett's players are readable and unread** — no cycle costs obtained (§3D).
- **★ The instrument measures ONE voice.** Multi-voice cost is not `n ×` anything until measured;
  a mixed-output player pays differently from n independent timers, and the GIME has one timer.
- **★ The 7-cycle spin constant** underlies every absolute figure (not the differences).
- **★ `ROOM.BIN` is now built and tested by nothing** — `room`/`walk` were its only consumers.
  Removing it is a disk-layout change and is **not** done here.
- Carried: the `MUSIC.SET*` mapping; no song decoded; `Demo` unbuilt; the cel bank does not
  survive the 16-colour mode; the two freezes; the mirrored chroma; the exit's +13%.
