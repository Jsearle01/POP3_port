### 1 — Summary

**PHASE 1 — SOLVED, from source, without needing the probe.** The Orchestrator's `FCharX` lead was right, and
the rule turned out to be **stated explicitly in the oracle** rather than something to infer
[`CTRLSUBS.S:805-838`]:

> ```
> lda Fcheck
> eor FCharFace       ; Look only at the hibits
> bmi :ok             ; They don't match --> even X-coord
>                     ; They match       --> odd X-coord
> ```

**The draw X is DOUBLED before that +1** — `FCharX = 2*(CharX + Fdx − ScrnLeft)` — so the doubled term is always
even and **`CharX` cannot affect parity at all.** The rule is:

```
ODD  iff  bit7(Fcheck) == bit7(CharFace)
```

**Both characters are explained**, and the thing that misled two dispatches is now visible: `Fcheck=$80` and
`CharFace=$FF` for both, so both are ODD — and **the vizier's apparent derivation from `CharX=197` was
coincidence.** 197 is odd; the rule independently says odd. One agreeing case is not a rule, and this one
agreed for two dispatches while everyone hunted cel geometry that could never have explained it.

**The prediction matters more than the fix: parity is a per-CEL property.** `Fcheck` varies between cels of the
*same* character. `Vwalk` runs ODD, ODD, ODD, **EVEN, EVEN**, ODD; `Vexit`, `Vraise` and `Palert` are also
internally mixed. **A bulk conversion at one parity per character is wrong for a third of the vizier's own walk
frames** — exactly the conversion volume piece C needs.

**The princess's empirical flag is retired.** Her cel is now converted at the derived odd column (125) instead
of `start_col=120 --flip-parity`, and the two outputs are **byte-identical** — which both proves the rule and
closes P3.22's "mechanism unresolved" flag. Suite PASS, 0 bytes wrong at both captures: a provable no-op.

### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — The rule, and where it lives

`GETFRAMEINFO`'s caller builds the frame's effective draw position [`CTRLSUBS.S:795-838`]:

```
 lda Fdx
 jsr addcharx        ; A := CharX + Fdx
 sec
 sbc #ScrnLeft       ; different coord system   (ScrnLeft = 58, EQ.S:479)
 sta FCharX
 asl FCharX          ; X := 2X
 rol FCharX+1
 ...
 lda Fcheck
 eor FCharFace       ; Look only at the hibits
 bmi :ok             ; They don't match --> even X-coord
 lda FCharX          ; They match       --> odd X-coord
 clc
 adc #1
```

`EOR` sets bit 7 when the bits **differ**, so `bmi` skips the `+1` on a mismatch — which matches the source's
own comments. Hence: **match → odd, mismatch → even**, and the `+1` is the *only* term that can be odd.

`FCharX ds 2` is two bytes because the value is doubled (and later sign-extended for off-screen positions), not
because it carries sub-byte precision. The Orchestrator's lead pointed at the right variable for a slightly
different reason than expected — the width is a consequence of the doubling, and the doubling is what makes
`CharX` irrelevant.

#### 3B — Why this went unfound for two dispatches

P3.22 observed the vizier's parity matching `CharX=197` and treated that as a derivation. It was a
**coincidence**, and a well-disguised one: the rule says ODD, and 197 is odd. Everything after that reasoned
from a false premise — P3.22 looked for what made the princess *different*, and P3.23 eliminated geometry,
mirroring and facing in a search whose framing was wrong from the start.

The eliminations were sound work and remain correct; they simply could not succeed, because the mechanism was
never in cel geometry. **What was missing was a disagreeing case.** A single cel with `Fcheck=$00` would have
broken the `CharX` story immediately — and there are several, sitting in the vizier's own walk cycle.

#### 3C — The predictive table, and its consequence

`cel_parity_rule.py` derives parity for the whole cutscene set. Verbatim, the sequences that are internally
mixed:

```
  Vwalk    48   $80   ODD    49 $80 ODD    50 $80 ODD
           51   $00   EVEN   52 $00 EVEN   53 $80 ODD
  Vexit    57-60 ODD   61 EVEN   62 ODD   63-64 EVEN   65 ODD   66 EVEN
  Vraise   85,67-70,72,73 EVEN   71,74,75,76,83,84 ODD
  Palert   2,3,4,6,8,9 ODD       5,7 EVEN
  MIXED PARITY WITHIN A SEQUENCE: Palert, Vexit, Vraise, Vwalk
```

So a conversion pipeline must take parity **per cel**, from that cel's `Fcheck` and the character's facing at
the time it is drawn. Note the second half of that: **parity depends on live facing**, so a cel drawn while the
character faces the other way flips parity. For this cutscene both characters hold `CharFace=−1` throughout, so
a static per-cel table is sufficient here — but that is a property of *this scene*, not a general one, and
gameplay will not have it.

`Fdx` is also non-zero for several `Vraise`/`Palert` cels (1, 2, 3, 5, −1), so position varies per frame too —
which the VM will need anyway for `chx`.

#### 3D — Retiring the empirical flag, with proof rather than assertion

P3.22 shipped the princess at `start_col=120 --flip-parity` on Jay's eye alone, flagged as mechanism-unknown.
The rule predicts her true column as `2*(120 − 58) + 1 = 125`, odd. Converting at 125 with **no flip** produces
output that is **byte-identical, all 44 rows**, to the shipped empirical version.

That is a two-way result: it confirms the rule against an independently-produced artifact, and it converts the
shipped cel from "right because Jay said so" to "right because the oracle says so". The suite re-ran green with
0 bytes wrong at both captures and both positions, so the substitution is a provable no-op — which is the only
reason it was safe to make without a fresh gate.

#### 3E — Why Phase 2 did not start, stated as my call

No hard-stop applied: §9.2 fires only when Phase 1 fails, and it succeeded. I stopped anyway, for reasons that
are about capacity rather than blockers:

- Piece C is substantial new 6809 — a cel-id table, the interpreter, the `NextFrame`/`FrameAdv` split, and the
  cadence plumbing. **P3.21 produced three bugs from writing new logic late in a session**, and this session is
  considerably longer than that one. The dispatch's own invariant says small and deliberate at phase boundaries.
- `prog` has ~174 bytes less headroom than documented (P3.22 §3B), so the VM very likely needs the bundle route
  from its first line. That is a structural decision worth making with room to verify it, not at the end.
- Phase 1's finding **changes Phase 2's input**: per-cel parity means the cel-id table wants a parity column,
  and the conversion step feeding it changes shape. Building the VM an hour before learning that would have

Handing over a solved rule beats handing over a half-built engine. Phase 2 is well-set: the rule is predictive
and tooled, the SPEED cadence is measured (frames 1:1; 13/5 `3,3,2,3,2` for SPEED 7; 6 for SPEED 12), and the

### 7 — Uncertainty flags

- **Parity depends on LIVE FACING, not only on the cel** (§3C). Both cutscene characters hold `CharFace=−1`
  throughout, so a static per-cel table works *here*. Gameplay, where characters turn, will need parity resolved
  at draw time or two variants per cel. This is a real scaling constraint and it is not solved by this dispatch.
- **Baked-in parity and turning are in tension.** P3.19's design freezes colour at conversion time; a character
  that turns changes parity. For the cutscene this never fires; for `Vexit` (which contains `aboutface` in the
  full sequence) it may.
- **`FCharX`'s exact units are not pinned.** `2*(CharX−58)` puts the vizier at 279 in a 280-wide space, which
  is suspicious for a character standing mid-room. Parity does not depend on it, so I did not chase it — but any
  future use of `FCharX` as an absolute column should verify the unit first.
- **`Fdx` is unmodelled in the current draw.** Several `Vraise`/`Palert` cels have non-zero `Fdx`; the P3.22
  slot records do not apply it. Phase 2 must, or those frames will sit one to five pixels off.
- **The rule is verified on two cels.** It explains both and predicts the rest, and the byte-identical check is
  strong for the princess — but no cel with `Fcheck=$00` has been rendered and eyeballed. `Pslump` (cel 1,
  EVEN) is the cheapest confirmation and is not yet done.
