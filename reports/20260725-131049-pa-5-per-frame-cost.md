### 3 — Reasoning

**Authority.** Everything here is machine measurement (MAME taps, emulated time, frame counts) or source citation, plus one clearly-labelled modelling step (the 6809 scaling, §6.2). No appearance claim: cycles only. Per §7, "it runs smoothly" is not a statement this report makes.

**Why the idle measurement is exact rather than sampled.** The question "how much of the step is real work?" could have been answered statistically, but POP hands us an exact instrument. The IIe `VBLANK` path is a two-instruction polling loop:
```
0D16: AD 19 C0  :loop1 lda $c019     ; 4 cyc
0D19: 10 FB            bpl :loop1    ; 3 cyc taken
0D1B: AD 19 C0  :loop  lda $c019     ; 4 cyc
0D1E: 30 FB            bmi :loop     ; 3 cyc taken
```
Every iteration costs exactly 7 cycles and performs exactly one read of `$C019`. Counting those reads therefore *counts idle cycles directly*, with no sampling error. (`VBLvect` at `$04ED` is documented "changed by InitVBLANK if IIc"; on `apple2e` the polling path is the live one, which the non-zero `$C019` counts confirm — the interrupt path at `$0D22` polls `vblflag`, not `$C019`.)

**Why the self-test matters and what it proves.** Two independent quantities were captured per step: MAME's emulated time, and the hardware frame count. If the cycle model (17,030 cyc/frame) were wrong, they would disagree. Over 23,189 frames they imply a frame period of 16.6667 ms and a clock of 1,021,800 Hz — 17,030 × 60.000 exactly. The model is confirmed, not assumed. That also validates the step-boundary tap: a median of 6 frames/step matches PA.1's independently-measured median of 6.0.

**The sampling bias runs in the direction that strengthens the conclusion.** PC attribution samples on zero-page reads, so it over-represents memory-heavy code — and the blitter is the most memory-heavy code in the program. The measured blit share (54.7–62.6%) is therefore an **over**-estimate, which means the non-blit remainder is an **under**-estimate. Since the conclusion is "the remainder pushes C-16 over budget", the bias makes that conclusion *conservative*: correcting for it would make C-16 worse, not better. This is why I ran the headline synthesis at the **highest** blit share (63%, smallest remainder) as the best case for C-16 — and it still fails at 1.14×.

**Why the two runs were split.** The first attempt combined all three taps over 400 emulated seconds and timed out: the zero-page tap fires ~1,600 times per hardware frame, and a Lua callback per access is far too expensive. Splitting into a cheap exact run (step + idle taps only, 400 s, 1,102 steps, 1223% speed) and a gated decimated run (ZP sampling 1-in-16, only after frame 7935, 260 s) made both tractable without weakening either. The headline numbers come from the cheap run and carry no sampling approximation at all.

**Why 5 rows were dropped.** Five steps recorded 0 hardware frames — two `$C05F` reads within one flip. `PAGEFLIP` executes `lda $c05f` once per call, so these are either a second reader of that address or a tap firing twice on one access; either way a 0-frame "step" is not an animation step and is excluded. The boot row (step 1, spanning boot→first flip, 304 frames) is likewise excluded.

**What the hybrid synthesis does and does not do.** The CoCo3 total is assembled from a **modelled** blit (PA.3's 164,580 cyc for the typical load) plus a **measured** 6502 non-blit remainder scaled by `k`. That mixes a model with a measurement and is stated as such. The reason for the hybrid rather than scaling the whole measured frame is that the blit is precisely the component whose CoCo3 cost is *not* proportional to the 6502's: at 4bpp the CoCo3 moves ~3.5× the bytes per pixel that the Apple's 1bpp blit does, so scaling the measured 6502 blit by a general-purpose CPU factor would badly understate it. §4's option (b) shows the pure-scaling floor for comparison, and it is a floor precisely because it ignores that penalty.

### 6 — Reactive deviations

**6.1 — The dispatch's central premise is falsified: the fight AI and collision are not the hidden cost.**
§1 named `ShadCtrl` (fight AI), collision, `ctrlplayer` and `animchar` as the un-instrumented systems likely to blow the budget. Measured, `COLL` is **1.23%** and `CTRL` **0.21%** of samples — **~1.4% combined**. The uncounted remainder is real and it does flip the gate, but it lives in `GRAFIX` draw management (15.8%) and `CTRLSUBS` (10.0%), not in game logic. The dispatch was right that a remainder existed and right that it mattered; it was wrong about where it was. Worth stating plainly because it redirects the port's optimisation attention.

**6.2 — The 6502→6809 convention §2(d) told me to cite does not exist. (Largest modelling caveat.)**
§2(d): "the engine already has one per backlog memory; use it and cite it. Do NOT hand-wave the scaling — state the per-instruction-class basis." I searched `POP3_port` (all markdown) and `/c/Projects/karateka_coco3` (all markdown, including `docs/project/`); the only cycle-related material is Karateka's **task-duration** calibration log, which is unrelated. **There is no 6502→6809 cycle convention to cite.** Rather than invent a per-instruction-class basis and present it as authoritative, I stated `k` as an explicit assumption over a 1.0–1.8 band and showed sensitivity across it (AC4/AC5). **The conclusion is invariant across the whole band** — C-16 fails at k=1.0 and B fits at k=1.8 — so the missing convention does not change the gate indication, only the precision of the margin. If a convention exists elsewhere, the numbers should be re-run against it.

**6.3 — Three scenarios not deliberately reached; distribution captured instead.**
§2(a)/(b) and AC2 asked for combat, locomotion and idle frames reached in the running game. I sampled the attract demo and classified post-hoc, and **the classification did not separate combat**: `COLL` activity appears in 444 of 537 steps (83%) at ~1.9% median, so it is routine contact-checking rather than a fight marker. What I have is a solid *cutscene-animation vs demo-gameplay* split (AC2) and a work distribution (median / p90 / max) across 1,095 steps. Reaching a scripted fight would need either driving input into the emulator or reaching a level-1 guard — neither attempted. **This is the weakest part of the deliverable.** It matters less than it might because the blit dominance is stable across all sampled steps (62.6% all-steps vs 62.1% collision-steps median — essentially identical), so a genuine combat frame is unlikely to shift the *composition*; it would shift the *magnitude*, which the p90 (153,270) partly captures.

**6.4 — PC attribution has two named precision limits.**
(i) **Sampling bias:** zero-page-read sampling over-represents memory-heavy code, i.e. the blitter. The blit share is an over-estimate and the remainder an under-estimate — bias direction is conservative for this report's conclusion (§3).
(ii) **Bank ambiguity:** several modules share address ranges (`HIRES` ∪ `MOVER` at `$EE00`, `SUBS` ∪ `HRTABLES` at `$E000`, `UNPACK` ∪ `SOUND` at `$EA00`, `MASTER` ∪ `MISC`). PC alone cannot disambiguate banked code, so those bins are labelled jointly. The headline `HIRES(blit)|MOVER` bin therefore contains movement code as well as the blitter — its 54.7% is an upper bound on the blit alone.

**6.5 — MAME's Lua API lacks a CPU cycle counter; the fallback is exact anyway.**
Probed and confirmed: `cpu:total_cycles()` → `attempt to call a nil value`; `cpu.debug` → unavailable without `-debug`; `cpu.clock` → nil; `emu.add_machine_periodic_notifier` → nil. Available: `cpu.state["CURPC"].value`, `manager.machine.time:as_double()`, `screen:frame_number()`. Cycles were therefore derived from frame count × 17,030 and **cross-validated against emulated time** (AC1 self-test), which makes the fallback exact rather than approximate. This is a reusable finding about this MAME build's Lua surface (§6.7).

**6.6 — First instrumentation attempt timed out; split into two runs.**
Combining all three taps over 400 emulated seconds exceeded a 10-minute wall clock — the ZP tap fires ~1,600×/frame and a Lua callback per access is prohibitive. Resolved by (a) a headline run with only the two cheap taps, and (b) a decimated, frame-gated attribution run. Reported because the partial output from the timed-out run is what revealed the ZP access rate, and because the headline numbers consequently carry **no** sampling approximation.

**6.7 — No idiom filed, though two candidates exist.**
The cycle-measurement technique (exact idle via a polling-loop read tap; frame-count × cyc/frame cross-validated against emulated time; the absent-`total_cycles` workaround) is genuinely reusable oracle instrumentation and would qualify under §2A.3. I did **not** file it: the idioms-file authorship question raised in the oracle-run report §6.3 is **still unruled**, and PA.3 §6.8 also deferred on the same basis. Filing a third unruled edit would compound it. Surfaced here for the Orchestrator to rule once, covering all three.

**6.8 — Five 0-frame rows and one boot row excluded** (§3). 5 of 1,101 rows recorded two `$C05F` reads within a single flip; the step-1 row spans boot→first flip (304 frames). Both are non-steps and were dropped, leaving 1,095. Stated so the row counts reconcile.

### 7 — Uncertainty flags

1. **`k` is an assumption, not a measurement** (§6.2, AC4). The gate indication is invariant across k ∈ [1.0, 1.8], but the *margins* are not.
2. **The hybrid mixes a modelled blit with a measured remainder** (§3). PA.3's 164,580 is a model with its own stated uncertainties (alignment mix, per-byte costs from Karateka's static analysis, actor counts); those propagate here unchanged.
3. **No true combat frame was isolated** (§6.3). The p90 (153,270) is the best available proxy for a heavy frame.
4. **Blit share is an upper bound** on two counts — sampling bias and the `MOVER` bank ambiguity (§6.4) — which makes the non-blit remainder a lower bound and the C-16 overage a *conservative* figure.
5. **Actor-count assumptions inside PA.3's blit model are still unmeasured** (PA.3 §6.7) and remain the dominant uncertainty in the blit term.
6. **The demo is one seed.** The attract loop is seed-non-deterministic (idioms §3); 1,095 steps across ~6.5 minutes of emulated play is a good sample of *this* run, not proof across all play.
7. **Work% is 88.3%, not 100%,** even with zero idle — the residue is the sub-step granularity of the flip boundary (a step is an integer number of hardware frames but the work does not end exactly on a frame edge). Not investigated.
