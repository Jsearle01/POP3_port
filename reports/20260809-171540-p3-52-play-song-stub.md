### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — §1: does the stub have to animate? No, and here is the evidence

The dispatch was right to make this a question rather than an assumption. Three things settle it:

1. **The port's holds are already pure waits.** `hold_frames` is `cmpy #0 / HAL_time_vbl_wait / leay -1,y` —
   it draws nothing.
2. **What the oracle's song loop drives does not exist on these screens.** `PlaySongI`'s loop runs
   `pburn / pstars / pflow` — the **princess room's** torches, stars and hourglass, drawn at fixed coordinates
   (`ptorchx db 13,25`, `ptorchy db 113,113`) from scene state the intro never initialises. The intro's six
   beats are a splash, three captions over it, and two prologue pictures.
3. **The regression holds all twelve checkpoints byte-identical**, including three "removal is exact" checks
   which would not survive anything moving underneath a caption.

**So the frames are spent waiting, which is both faithful and simpler.** `play_song` falls through into
`hold_frames` deliberately — one loop that spends frames, rather than two that could drift apart. If a beat
ever gains a live element, it is driven from inside `play_song` and every caller inherits it.

#### 3B — HARD-STOP #2: the two numbers measure different things

`SUBS.S:822-829`:

```
PlaySongI
 tay / lda soundon / and musicon / bne :1
 txa / beq ]rts / jmp play        <-- X spent here ONLY when sound is off
:1 tya / jsr minit / jsr swpage
:loop ... jsr mplay / cmp #0 / bne :loop     <-- otherwise the SONG sets the length
```

**With sound on — the oracle's normal state — `X` is never read.** The beat lasts as long as `mplay` keeps
returning non-zero. That is the duration P3.3/P3.4 measured off the running machine and put in the beat table,
and it is what the port reproduces today.

**Both numbers are legitimate and they are not interchangeable.** Which one the port should honour is a
fidelity judgement — reproduce the oracle *as normally run*, or as it runs with music disabled — and the
hard-stop is right that it is not mine to make. **I changed no duration.** Each row now carries the oracle's
X in its comment, so the question is visible at the point of decision instead of needing this report to
surface it.

#### 3C — The structure, and why `BEAT_SONG` rather than five call sites

The oracle has five `PlaySongI` sites; the port has **one table-driven beat loop**, so transplanting five
calls would have meant unrolling a mechanism to match a shape the port does not have. `BEAT_SONG` puts the
same fact in the port's own idiom: **the beat record now records what its hold IS.**

That is exactly the distinction the dispatch asked for — *"a reader cannot tell a song's length from a
designed pause"* — and it turns out **one of the six is genuinely a pause**: beat 5's 1,564 frames carry
`BEAT_SONG 0`, because the oracle's gap there is the PrincessScene cutscene, which is not built. Before this
change that beat was indistinguishable from the five that are songs. **It is now the only row with a zero, and
that is legible at a glance.**

`BEAT_PRE` is deliberately **not** routed through `play_song`: the pre-hold is the clean base shown before the
caption, which is a designed interval and not a song. Both distinctions now live in the data.

**Mechanics:** the record grew 8 → 9 bytes. `BEAT_SIZE` has exactly one consumer (`leax BEAT_SIZE,x`), so the
non-power-of-two is harmless — checked before changing it rather than after.

#### 3D — Verification of "invisible on screen"

HARD-STOP #3 says the durations must not change, so they are **measured, not asserted**. The regression logs
its own frame numbers:

| beat | log | measured | table |
|---|---|---|---|
| presents | f1300 → f1582 | **282** | 281 + 1 reveal swap |
| byline | f1679 → f1963 | **284** | 283 + 1 |

**Twelve of twelve offline comparisons byte-identical**, at **both** memory sizes. The change is structural.
