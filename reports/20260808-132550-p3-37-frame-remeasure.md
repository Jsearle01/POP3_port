### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — Same method, so the two numbers differ only by the restructure

P3.19's loaded model charged: a counted row loop (5 cy/row), mode transitions (10 cy × 3/row), skip-run
pointer advances (5 cy × 2/row), per-call setup of 40 cy × **3 calls/character** (erase + save + draw), across
vizier 48×9B, princess 43×5B, two flames, hourglass and four stars — **19,579 cy**, reported against the
**tightest of its three budgets, 29,673** (29,859 minus the 186 cy page flip).

**I have matched that budget choice**, so the comparison is like for like.

**The per-cel and per-character components are untouched by this dispatch**: the same cels, the same widths,
the same rates. What changed is only the frame's *control structure*, so only that is recounted.

#### 3B — What three passes actually cost, and why it is nearly nothing

Two facts do the work, and both cut against the intuition that more passes cost more:

**One — `char_one` is still called six times.** Three passes × two characters is the same six calls as two
characters × three phases. **The 40 cy × 6 = 240 cy of per-call setup is identical**; the calls were reordered,
not multiplied. The restructure moved *when* work happens, not *how much*.

**Two — the moved test moved out of `char_one` and into `ch_scan`, and that is a net removal.** It used to run
on every call (6×/frame); it now runs once per character in the scan (2×/frame). **Four fewer evaluations**,
which nearly pays for the new dispatch overhead.

Counted from the code as committed:

| added | cy |
|---|---|
| `vm_frameadv`: `jsr ch_scan` + 3×(`lda`,`bsr`) + `rts` | 40 |
| `ch_scan` dispatch | 43 |
| `ch_scan_one` ×2 | 74 |
| `ch_index` ×2 (now called from the scan) | 60 |
| `ch_moved` ×2 | 110 |
| `ch_pass` dispatch ×3, less the one character loop it replaced | 86 |
| **subtotal** | **413** |

| removed | cy |
|---|---|
| `ch_index` + `ch_moved`: 6 evaluations → 2 (net −4) | **340** |

**Net +73 cy.**

**And the frame-wide peel-skip cannot make the heaviest frame heavier.** P3.32 made the skip a property of the
frame — if anything moved, everybody peels — which makes *more frames* heavy. But **P3.19's model already
charged erase + save + draw for both characters**, i.e. it already assumed nobody skips. The heaviest frame was
already at maximum, so a more conservative skip has nothing left to add to it. That is the single largest
reason the baseline held, and it is a property of what P3.19 chose to model rather than of P3.32's change.

#### 3C — Two component costs are estimated, and the result is insensitive to both

`ch_index` (~30 cy) and `ch_moved` (~55 cy) are **estimated from their instruction counts, not counted
instruction by instruction.** That is a weaker basis than the rest of the arithmetic and I am not going to
present it as equal.

It does not matter here, because the two appear on **both** sides of the ledger — twice added in the scan,
six times removed from `char_one` — so an error in either largely cancels. Sweeping them across plausible
ranges:

| `ch_moved` | `ch_index` | baseline | threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 20 | 19,702 | 28.0 |
| 55 | 30 | 19,652 | 28.1 |
| 80 | 45 | 19,572 | 28.4 |

**The threshold moves between 28.0 and 28.4 across the whole range** — and the loop is 30.0 at its most
favourable. **No plausible value of either estimate changes the verdict**, which is what makes the estimate
acceptable rather than a hole.

### 5 — What the number means

**The gap survives, and it is now measured against current structure.** At the all-in rate the shifted path
needs **12,104 cy** and has **10,021** — short by **2,083**, about 17%.

P3.36's levers, restated with the corrected numbers:

- **Fewer bytes in the heaviest frame** — **147 B/cel** required at 34.0 cy/byte, against the measured 178.
  (P3.36 said 148; the re-measure moves it by one byte.) A 17% reduction.
- **More time** — the frame structure. **This dispatch closes off the cheap version of that lever:** the
  three-pass restructure is not carrying meaningful overhead, so there is no easy 2,000 cy to reclaim by
  reorganising passes. Any time lever now has to come from the draw itself.
- **More space** — banking, or the staged loading **Jay deferred rather than closed**.

**I have pulled none of them**, and per HARD-STOP #3 I did not proceed further even though the measurement
came out decisive.

**The one thing this dispatch adds to the decision:** the "more time" lever is weaker than it looked. That is
a real narrowing, and it makes the choice more clearly between **fewer bytes** and **more space**.
