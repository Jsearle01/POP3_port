### 3 — Reasoning

#### 3A — What was built, including the part that is easy to assume away

Per row, the span from the first to the last non-transparent byte; shift only that span.

**The edge growth is handled explicitly.** A span of *n* bytes shifted right by *k* pixels occupies *n+1*
bytes and **both ends are partial**: the shift pushes pixels out of the last byte into a new one, and the
first byte's low pixels are untouched background. So the two edge bytes must be **merged** with the
destination while the interior may be stored.

One property makes a span shiftable independently of its row, and it is worth stating because the design
rests on it: **the byte immediately before a span is transparent by construction** — that is what "first
non-transparent byte" means — so it contributes no carry into the span's first byte. A span can be shifted
starting from a zero carry.

#### 3B — Verification, and its provenance

The truth source is the **baked phase-k cels**, produced by `cel_blit_prep.py` shifting in **pixel space** and
repacking. This shifts a **byte span** with a table and a carry. **Neither derives from the other.**

**18 cel/phase combinations, 0 mismatched.**

That independence is the check's entire value: five of this project's six silent instrument failures shared an
input with their subject, and the most recent cost three ablations across two sessions.

#### 3C — What (b) actually costs, and what the alternatives would buy

| cel | footprint | span | drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| vwalk48 | 282 | 208 | 151 |
| vwalk51 | 470 | 253 | 202 |
| **mean** | **376** | **232** | **178** |

(b) recovers most of the transparent waste — 376 → 232 — because the empty margins around a figure are at the
row ends, which is exactly what a span excludes. What it cannot recover is the **interior** transparency: gaps
*within* the figure's horizontal extent, between an arm and a torso. That is **54 B/cel, 23% of the span**.

**So the value of (a) and (c) is now a number rather than a preference: 54 bytes per cel per draw.** They
differ in where they pay for it — (a) re-derives segments at runtime, (c) moves the work to bake time and
changes the storage figure — but they buy the same 54 bytes.

#### 3D — Why the floor settles it, and how much margin the survivors have

The 21 cy/byte is a **counted floor**, not an estimate:

```
  lda ,x+     6      the source byte
  ldb a,y     5      SHR[src] — the in-byte part
  orb <carry  4      the previous byte's spill
  stb ,u+     6      store
             21      and this omits: the SHL lookup for the NEXT carry,
                     storing it, and the loop control
```

Everything omitted is work the loop must do, so **the true rate is strictly above 21.** Sensitivity:

| design | 21 (floor) | 25 | 30 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (b) per-row spans | 99% | 105% | 113% | 121% |
| (a)/(c) drawn only | 91% | 96% | 102% | 108% |

**(b) is over at every rate above the floor**, and the floor is unreachable by construction. **(a)/(c) hold to
roughly 24 cy/byte** — a real but narrow margin, and whether the complete loop lands under it is the open
question.

I have not written the complete loop, so I am not reporting a rate for it. The 25/30/35 columns are
sensitivity, explicitly labelled, not a prediction of where it lands.

#### 3E — What this does to the representation choice

Space (P3.33) eliminated pre-shifted for this scene: 23,506 B against a 14,848 B window. Time now eliminates
(b). The state:

| candidate | space | time |
|---|---|---|
| pre-shifted | **over by 8,658** | 66%, measured |
| shifted, (b) spans | fits, 10,454 | **over — 99% at a strict floor** |
| shifted, (a)/(c) | fits, but (c) changes the figure | 91% at the floor; **undetermined above it** |

**No candidate has yet cleared both budgets**, and the survivors' margin is ~3 cy/byte wide. That is a real
result and I am reporting it rather than tuning toward one — HARD-STOP #3.

**One thing the hybrid needs said, because it is easy to over-credit.** It pre-shifts single-phase cels and
runtime-shifts two-phase ones — but the **heaviest frame is heaviest because both characters are moving**, and
moving cels are the two-phase ones. So in the frame that decides the budget, the hybrid pays close to the full
shift cost and its saving is largely elsewhere. Its value is in space and in the average frame, not in the
constraint being measured.
